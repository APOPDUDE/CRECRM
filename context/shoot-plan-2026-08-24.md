## Stop 1 - Hyde Park Village, 7:30 - 8:25

Six blocks of brick walkways, live oaks, and the 1983 Village Circle fountain, between Swann and
Rome. At 7:30 on a Monday the retail is closed and the streets are empty. Treat that as the point,
not the problem: the shot is a broker working while the neighborhood that shops here is still
asleep. Do not try to fake a bustling Village at 7:30. It will not read.

### Questions to answer on camera

Pick two or three. Answer them the way you would answer them to a friend, not to a lens.

**On the early start**
- What time did you actually start today, and what did you do before this camera turned on?
- Who else in this business is awake right now?
- What does the first hour of your day buy you that the rest of the day cannot?

**On the 10,000 calls video (the listicle)**
- How many calls before you got a real conversation? Not a deal, a conversation.
- What were you saying at call 100 that you would never say now?
- What is the one thing you would tell someone sitting at call 40 about to quit?
- Which of the 10,000 actually turned into a closed deal, and how long did that take?
- What did you stop saying?
- What time of day actually connects, and how did you figure that out?
- What can 10,000 calls teach you that no course can?

The CTA mechanic from that reel is the DM bait. It needs one keyword. Say the number out loud in
the first three seconds and end with a single instruction: comment CALLS and the five go out by text.
Say the keyword once, clearly, and do not bury it.

**On specialization** (film this here, in the polished setting, because the contrast with the
warehouse footage later is the whole edit)
- What did you turn down, and what did it cost you at the time?
- What does an investor ask you now that they would never ask a generalist?
- How long before "the industrial guy" started bringing you inbound instead of you chasing it?
- What is the honest downside of specializing?
- If you started over in Tampa tomorrow, what would you pick and why?

Your own line - "investors want best in class, not a man of many talents" - is the hook. Say it
once, cold, straight to camera, in the first four seconds. Do not build up to it.

### Questions to answer while driving

**On the AI / Claude workflow** (this is the strongest of the three, film it first)
- What did your lead tracking look like a year ago, and what does it look like this morning?
- What did you find out about a property this week that you would have missed last year?

## The reusable B-roll bank

This is the bucket that matters most for your ask - footage the editor can cut new videos from on
weeks nobody can record you. None of it is tied to a specific script. Shoot it whenever there is a
gap, and shoot more of it than feels necessary.

**Working footage** (any location, any day)
- Typing, three angles: over the shoulder, from the side, top-down on hands
- Phone to ear, standing, walking, sitting - at least 60 seconds total
- Writing in a notebook, tight on the pen
- Closing a laptop and standing up
- Scrolling a listing on a phone, screen legible
- Coffee and laptop, static, 20 seconds

**Movement footage**
- Walking toward camera, away from camera, past camera - 5 takes each
- Getting in and out of the car - 4 takes
- Opening and closing a warehouse door
- Walking a property line, wide, small in frame

**Property texture** (get this at every building you visit from now on, permanently)
- Dock doors, truck courts, yards, fencing, trailers, gravel
- Ceiling and clear height from below
- Signage and address numbers
- Wide establishing shots with sky

**Screen recordings** (record these yourself, no videographer needed)
- Your CRM pipeline with real deals moving through it
- A LoopNet or Crexi search being run
- A Claude conversation where you are actually working a lead
- Your calendar for a real week

Screen recordings are the highest-leverage thing on this list. They cost nothing, they need nobody
else present, and they carry any AI or workflow video by themselves.

## Technical notes

- **Shoot vertical-safe.** 4K, framed so a 9:16 crop works, so the same footage can go to Reels,
  Shorts, and LinkedIn.
- **24fps for talking, 60fps for B-roll.** Everything on the B-roll lists above should be 60.
- **Lav on Alex all morning.** Non-negotiable on Adamo and in the car. Record a backup track on the
  camera.
- **30 seconds of room tone at every stop.** Takes no time, saves the edit.
- **Expose for the face, not the sky.** Every exterior this morning is a backlight problem.
- **One outfit all morning.** Four locations have to cut together as one day.
- **Say the hook in the first three seconds** of every take. If the take starts with a wind-up,
  reset and go again. That is the only rule worth interrupting a natural answer for.
- **Slate each take out loud** with the topic - "calls, take two." It will save an hour in the edit.

## Optional block after 12:00 PM

Only if the morning slips. Two things to know:

- **Solar noon is 1:32 PM.** At 12:00 the sun is roughly 63 degrees up. Flat, overhead, unflattering.
  Anything shot outdoors between noon and 3:00 will look worse than everything you shot at 7:30, and
  it will not cut together with it.
- **August in Tampa means afternoon storms.** A 12:00 to 1:30 block is reasonably safe. Anything
  after 2:00 is a gamble.

So the afternoon block is **interiors only**: the Hyde House photo studio if it is bookable, inside a
warehouse, or parked in shade in the car. Do not schedule an outdoor pickup for the afternoon.
